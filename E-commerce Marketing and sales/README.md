# E-commerce Marketing and Sales Analytics

## 📌 Project Overview

The e-commerce company aims to leverage **data-driven insights** to improve customer acquisition, retention, revenue optimization, and overall business performance.

This project analyzes customer transactions, purchasing behavior, product performance, customer segments, cohorts, seasonality, and sales trends to answer key business questions and provide actionable insights for marketing, sales, inventory, and customer-retention strategies.

---

## 🎯 Business Objective

The primary objectives of this project are to:

- Understand customer acquisition trends.
- Identify high and low acquisition periods.
- Analyze customer retention behavior.
- Compare revenue generated by new and existing customers.
- Identify top-performing products.
- Perform **RFM-based customer segmentation**.
- Analyze revenue contribution by customer segment.
- Perform cohort-based retention analysis.
- Calculate and analyze customer lifetime value (CLV/LTV).
- Identify seasonal trends across product categories and locations.
- Analyze daily sales performance.
- Provide actionable business recommendations.

|---|---|

| **Monetary** | How much revenue the customer generated |

# 📊 7. Cohort Retention Analysis

### Question

Group customers based on their **month of first purchase** and analyze retention over time.

### Cohort Definition

Each customer is assigned to a cohort based on the month in which they made their first purchase.

Example:

```text
January 2025 Cohort
February 2025 Cohort
March 2025 Cohort
...
```

### Analysis

Calculate retention rates across subsequent months.

Example:

| Cohort | Month 0 | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan-25 | 100% | 45% | 32% | 25% |
| Feb-25 | 100% | 50% | 36% | 29% |
| Mar-25 | 100% | 42% | 28% | 21% |

### Business Questions

Identify:

- Highest-retention cohorts
- Lowest-retention cohorts
- Retention drop-off periods
- Cohorts affected by specific acquisition campaigns

### Business Recommendations

For weaker cohorts:

- Introduce post-purchase engagement campaigns.
- Provide second-purchase incentives.
- Use personalized recommendations.
- Introduce loyalty programs.
- Implement abandoned-cart and reactivation campaigns.
- Improve onboarding and customer experience.

# 📊 RFM Methodology

The RFM analysis should follow these steps:

### Step 1 — Calculate Recency

Calculate the number of days since the customer's most recent purchase.

```text
Recency = Analysis Date - Last Purchase Date
```

### Step 2 — Calculate Frequency

Count the number of purchases/orders made by each customer.

```text
Frequency = Number of Orders
```

### Step 3 — Calculate Monetary Value

Calculate the total revenue generated by each customer.

```text
Monetary = Total Customer Revenue
```

### Step 4 — Assign Scores

Customers can be scored using quantiles or business-defined thresholds.

Example:

```text
RFM Score = Recency Score + Frequency Score + Monetary Score
```

### Step 5 — Create Customer Segments

Example segments:

```text
Premium
Gold
Silver
Standard
```

The final thresholds should be determined based on the dataset rather than assumed values.

# 📊 Cohort Analysis Methodology

### Cohort Month

For each customer:

```text
Cohort Month = Month of First Purchase
```

### Purchase Month

```text
Purchase Month = Month of Transaction
```

### Cohort Index

```text
Cohort Index = Months Since First Purchase
```

Retention is then calculated as:

```text
Retention Rate =
Active Customers in Cohort Period
/
Customers in Original Cohort
```

The resulting matrix can be visualized using a retention heatmap.

# 💰 Customer Lifetime Value

Where sufficient historical information is available, estimate customer lifetime value using:

```text
LTV ≈
Average Order Value
× Purchase Frequency
× Expected Customer Lifespan
```

Alternatively, a historical LTV measure can be calculated as:

```text
Historical LTV =
Total Revenue Generated by Customer
```

The selected methodology should be clearly documented based on the available dataset.

# ⚠️ Important Analytical Considerations

The analysis should avoid making unsupported assumptions.

Particular attention should be given to:

- Missing values
- Duplicate transactions
- Cancelled orders
- Refunds
- Returns
- Outliers
- Incomplete customer histories
- Date inconsistencies
- New customers with limited observation periods

Retention and LTV calculations should account for the available observation window to avoid misleading comparisons between recent and older cohorts.
